MIT sources and distinct coverage

This guide collects substantive MIT-hosted accounts of STAR, its tools, and the people profiled in this archive. The linked articles and reports remain on MIT websites; their full text is not reproduced here. It is a researched selection as of October 2, 2026, not an exhaustive index of every page on mit.edu. Routine StarCluster documentation and mailing-list messages are already part of the site archive and are not repeated here.

Reporting and interviews

  • Protein viewer unveiled (MIT News, 2006) — the PDBViewer collaboration that preceded StarBiochem; names Ivica Ceraj and quotes Chuck Shubert.
  • Think small! Think quickly! Atomistic model helps students visualize nanoscale problems (MIT News, 2007) — Ivica Ceraj and Markus Buehler’s StarGP interface for atomistic modeling.
  • MIT awarded three HHMI grants to promote excellence in science education (MIT News, 2010) — Graham Walker’s support for continuing development and wider use of StarBiochem and StarGenetics.
  • STAR team recognized for impact on STEM education in Massachusetts high schools (MIT News, 2012) — award, classroom use, and named work by Chuck Shubert, Ivica Ceraj, Lourdes Alemán, Alison Brauneis, Sara Bonner, and Stacie Bumgarner.
  • OEIT’s role in fostering open education (MIT News, 2012) — Vijay Kumar describes STAR’s role in bringing research tools into teaching.
  • DeGraff awarded NSF grant for Haiti linguistics research (MIT News, 2012) — plans to translate STAR tools into Kreyòl for biology classes.
  • Q&A: Michel DeGraff on teaching STEM in Kreyòl (MIT News, 2012) — discusses STAR as a model for open educational resources in Haitian Creole.
  • Haitian educators and MIT faculty develop Kreyòl-based teaching tools (MIT News, 2015) — reports translated STAR tools; its team photograph identifies Lourdes Alemán and Alison Brauneis.

MIT project accounts and teaching materials

  • StarBiochem case study (OEIT) — origins, teaching use, and credits for Chuck Shubert and Ivica Ceraj.
  • StarGenetics case study (OEIT) — simulation of genetic crosses and Ivica Ceraj’s contributor credit.
  • StarORF project page (OEIT) — open reading frame teaching tool; credits Ivica Ceraj and Lourdes Alemán.
  • StarHydro project page (OEIT) — hydrology visualization and Justin Riley’s contributor credit.
  • StarMolsim case study (OEIT) — describes StarGP as its predecessor and Justin Riley as lead architect.
  • Star Molsim suite joins NanoHUB environment (OEIT, 2011) — Justin Riley’s interface update and migration work.
  • Using StarCluster: A Toolbox for Managing Amazon Elastic Compute Clouds (OEIT IAP listing, 2010) — Justin Riley’s class on StarCluster in teaching and research.
  • StarCellBio development (MIT Education Group) — biology and software collaborators, including Lourdes Alemán, Alison Brauneis, Ivica Ceraj, and Sara Bonner.
  • High school science education (MIT Education Group) — work by Lourdes Alemán and Stacie Bumgarner.
  • January 2013 MIT-Haiti workshop agenda — lists Lourdes Alemán and Alison Brauneis among biology workshop instructors.
  • StarCellBio project proposal (MIT-hosted PDF) — proposed teaching tool and named development collaborators.

Institutional reports and original writing

  • StarBiochem: A Role Model for Teaching the Sciences (MIT IS&T News, 2008) — early STAR history and Chuck Shubert and Ivica Ceraj’s roles.
  • Dean for Undergraduate Education, 2007–2008 (MIT Reports to the President) — records a DUE award for Shubert, Ceraj, and Riley.
  • Dean for Undergraduate Education, 2008–2009 — STAR staffing and projects, including Sara Bonner, Justin Riley, and Lourdes Alemán.
  • OEIT president’s report, 2009–2010 — lists the STAR software then supported.
  • Dean for Undergraduate Education, 2010–2011 — names Charles Shubert, Ivan Ceraj, Justin Riley, and Sara Bonner as the STAR group, with Lourdes Alemán and Stacie Bumgarner in the collaborating Education Group.
  • Department of Biology, 2013–2014 — reports classroom use of StarGenetics and StarBiochem.
  • Department of Biology, 2014–2015 — describes StarBiochem, StarGenetics, and StarCellBio as freely available teaching software.
  • Integrating protein visualization in the classroom with StarBiochem (2009 manuscript, locally archived from STAR).
  • Bringing Research Tools into the Classroom (Shubert, Ceraj, and Riley; locally archived from STAR).
  • StarHPC: Teaching Parallel Programming within Elastic Compute Cloud (Ceraj, Riley, and Shubert; locally archived from STAR).
